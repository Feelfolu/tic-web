Romania spends 0.07% of GDP on its culture ministry. Its creators want that doubled

Romania's creators' unions are petitioning for at least 0.15% of GDP for culture in the 2027 budget. What it means if you work with Romanian partners.

In short

  • Romania's national alliance of creators' unions (ANUC) is asking for at least 0.15% of GDP for the Ministry of Culture in 2027, roughly double today's share.
  • Today that comes to about 74 lei, roughly EUR 14–15, per resident a year.
  • The petition also asks counties and cities to raise their culture budgets. If you work with Romanian partners, that local money is often what makes co-financing possible.

The Romanian Ministry of Culture's budget has been frozen at 1.4 billion lei since 2024, a real-terms cut of more than 16%. In 2026 it amounts to 0.07% of GDP; the Romanian Cultural Institute's projects get 0.0007%. According to ANUC, Romania ranks last in the European Union.

On 2 November, ANUC launched a petition asking for at least 0.15% of GDP in the 2027 state budget, and a similar increase for the Romanian Cultural Institute. Signatories include the writers', architects', visual artists', filmmakers', composers' and theatre unions.

Why it matters for international projects

Most European funding needs a match. In Creative Europe cooperation projects, for example, the EU covers 60–80% of the budget depending on the project's scale, and each partner has to find the rest. For Romanian organisations, that share usually comes from the Ministry of Culture, from the National Cultural Fund Administration (AFCN), or from county and city budgets. When those are tight, Romanian partners can commit to less, or need longer to confirm.

The other side

Romania is still consolidating its public finances: the Fiscal Council expects a deficit of around 5.5% of GDP in 2027. The petitioners accept the need to cut the deficit, but argue that the sums involved are marginal in the overall budget. The government programme adopted in 2024 had itself promised to raise the ministry's budget every year, reaching 1% of GDP by 2028.

What to do now

  • If you are planning a project with Romanian partners: ask early where their co-financing will come from, and build in time. Local budgets are usually adopted early in the year, and most county and city culture calls closed between May and July in 2026.
  • If you want to follow the funding picture: our funding calendar lists the calls open right now.
  • If you support the petition: it is open here, in Romanian.

What we don't know yet

How much the government will allocate to culture in 2027: the draft budget is not ready yet. Nor whether counties and cities will respond to the call. We will follow up here.
